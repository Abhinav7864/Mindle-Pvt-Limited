## **DESCRIPTION OF THE INVENTION** 

## **TECHNICAL FIELD OF THE INVENTION** 

**1.** The present invention is in the area of mobile computing applications, artificial intelligence, natural language processing, and digital wellness technology. More specifically, it concerns an integrated iOS mobile application, referred to throughout this document as "GitaConnect" or "the System", built for systematic study of the Bhagavad Gita, daily spiritual practice, community participation, gamified self-improvement, and AI-guided spiritual mentorship. 

**2.** In particular, the invention involves the following: 

(a) An AI-powered multilingual spiritual mentor that uses a domain-restricted large language model combined with a proprietary anti-hallucination prompt engineering setup. 

(b) A mood assessment system with multiple steps that takes the user's emotional state and links it to relevant Bhagavad Gita verses through a structured versemapping data design. 

(c) A Spiritual Feed that shows users a vertically scrollable set of short video reels made by the development team, each tied to a specific Gita chapter and verse. Each reel has a like button, a share button, and an information ("i") button that opens a modal showing structured teachings for that chapter and verse. 

(d) A gamified Spiritual Challenges Engine that gives users daily, weekly, and milestone-based spiritual challenges across various practice types, each with clearly defined completion criteria. 

(e) The Home Screen displays a Mantra Library of 140 developer-provided mantras according to the day of the week; each day corresponds to a particular deity and its mantras. Similar to a music app, a mantra playback system allows users to search for, pause, play, and switch between mantras. Each repetition tap is tracked by a Digital Jaap counter, which provides you with haptic feedback in the form of a light pulse for each repetition and a heavier pulse for a complete mala. 

(f) An AI chat interface that initiates a conversation with the user based on the verse they are reading, eliminating the need for the user to enter the verse reference. 

(g) A spiritual analytics engine that examines a wide range of factors over a rolling ninety-day period, including reading, moods, mantra listening, Jaap counts, and challenge completions. 

## **BACKGROUND OF THE INVENTION** 

**1.** The Bhagavad Gita comprises 18 chapters and 700 Sanskrit verses. It addresses modern challenges of anxiety, purpose, ethics, and mental well-being, but meaningful engagement with the Gita remains hindered by barriers of language, the need for a teacher, and the absence of interactive digital tools that meet users where they are emotionally. 

**2.** Mobile applications are now the primary medium through which individuals engage with digital content for learning and self-improvement. However, existing applications in the spiritual and scriptural domain have significant limitations. Most Gita applications present the text as a static digital book, showing Sanskrit verses, transliterations, and translations without any interactivity or personalisation. The user is expected to arrive with pre-existing knowledge of which verse to read, when, and why. There is no mechanism to connect the user's present emotional state to the most relevant scripture for that moment. 

**3.** AI-driven chatbots have become widespread, but none have been deployed in a domainconstrained spiritual mentorship context that (a) restricts the AI's knowledge boundary to a specific canonical text, (b) enforces language lock to respond in the user's regional Indian language, (c) applies anti-hallucination safeguards verified against a device-resident scripture datastore, and (d) enables users to directly ask the AI about a specific verse they are presently reading. 

**4.** Community and gamification features, standard in language learning (e.g., Duolingo) and fitness applications (e.g., Strava), are missing from spiritual practice applications. Users engaging in scripture study, mantra repetition, or daily prayer have no mechanism for social recognition, streaks, challenges, or milestone achievements, all of which are established motivators of habit formation. This reduces long-term retention and engagement in spiritual practice apps. 

**5.** Feed-style content consumption, the paradigm popularised by social media applications, has not been applied to spiritual content. A user currently must navigate deep into menus to find a relevant verse or content card. There is no scrollable, algorithmically or editorially curated spiritual content surface that allows passive discovery of Gita teachings, devotional reflections. 

**6.** Statistical tracking of spiritual practice, analogous to fitness and habit-tracking applications, does not exist in any current Gita or spiritual application. Users who wish to monitor their reading speed, mantra practice consistency, mood trends over time, or challenge completion rates have no tools available to them. 

**7.** Thus, there exists a clear, unmet need in the art for an integrated platform that combines scriptural depth, AI-powered personalised guidance, emotional intelligence, community engagement, gamification, and data analytics in a single cohesive spiritual technology system designed for the Indian user.

## **SUMMARY OF THE INVENTION** 

**1.** This summary is provided to introduce a selection of concepts in simplified form. It is not intended to identify key features or essential features of the claimed subject matter, nor is it intended to be used as an aid in determining the scope of the claimed subject matter. 

**2.** The present invention provides GitaConnect, an integrated iOS mobile application system for Bhagavad Gita-based spiritual guidance, community engagement, and practice analytics, comprising seven primary inventive subsystems that together form a cohesive spiritual technology platform. 

**3.** The AI Spiritual Mentor Module is the First Inventive Subsystem. This module uses a cloud serverless function proxy to incorporate the Sarvam-30B large language model, an AI system created especially for Indian regional languages. The module is controlled by a proprietary multi-rule prompt engineering framework (SpiritualMentorPromptBuilder) that enforces the following at runtime: (i) language lock, which causes the model to respond in a user-selected Indian language regardless of the language of input; (ii) conversational tone calibration, which matches response depth to input depth; (iii) anti-hallucination constraints, which require the AI to cite only verses of the Bhagavad Gita that it is certain exist; (iv) a verse-reference extraction engine that uses dual regular expression patterns to parse responses to extract chapter and verse coordinates; and (v) verse validation against the device-resident scripture datastore before display and (vi) a multi-stage output sanitisation pipeline stripping internal reasoning blocks, markdown symbols, and meta-content tags. The module supports eleven Indian languages: English, Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Kannada, Malayalam, Marathi, Gujarati, Bengali, Punjabi, and Odia. 

**4.** The Second Inventive Subsystem is the Mood-Based Verse Recommendation Engine. This subsystem conducts a three-step emotional assessment: (i) initial mood category selection; (ii) a continuous pleasantness intensity rating rendered as an animated, dynamically morphing SwiftUI shape (StateOfMindView) whose colour palette transitions through five states from red (Very Unpleasant) to yellow (Very Pleasant); and (iii) optional free-text journaling. The resulting pleasantness level is mapped through a structured JSON verse-mapping table to a curated set of Bhagavad Gita verse references. The day's assigned verse is persisted to local device storage and synchronised to a cloud database table, with a one-per-day constraint preventing re-prompting on subsequent launches that day. 

**5.** The Third Inventive Subsystem is the Spiritual Feed Module. This subsystem presents a vertically scrollable reel-based video feed (FeedViewController) populated with developercreated video content tied to specific Bhagavad Gita chapters and verses.The development team of the application creates and selects all of the video content; user-generated content is not supported. Each reel has three features: (i) an information ("i") button that opens a modal overlay with structured lessons related to the chapter and verse featured in that reel; (ii) a share affordance for sharing reel content via the native iOS share sheet; and (iii) a hearttoggle-like interaction that is persisted to the user's liked items collection. The main daily discovery surface is the Feed module, which enables both active learning engagement and passive consumption of Bhagavad Gita content. 

**6.** The Gamified Spiritual Challenges Engine is the fourth inventive subsystem. This subsystem uses a structured challenge framework that includes: (i) daily challenges (e.g., read one chapter verse, complete one mala, log today's mood, listen to one mantra) that reset every 24 hours; (ii) weekly challenges (e.g., read all verses of a particular chapter, complete seven days of mood logging, complete a target number of malas) with seven-day windows; (iii) milestone challenges awarded upon reaching cumulative activity thresholds (e.g., 10 malas completed, 30-day reading streak); and (iv) an achievement badge gallery. The StatsStore and ReadingProgressManager show you how far you've come in real time for each challenge, like "3 of 5 verses read today." When you finish a challenge, it stays in both the cloud and your local storage. As you keep completing challenges, your Spiritual Level score and streak 

counter both go up. 

**7.** The Fifth Creative Subsystem is made up of the Mantra Library, the Playback System, and the Digital Jaap Counter.The three components that make up this subsystem are extremely similar to one another. A curated Mantra Library of 140 developer-provided mantras is first displayed on the Home Screen by a day-based filtering system. Every day of the week has a different deity and set of mantras. This makes sure that the mantras on the Home Screen are right for that day. You can see all 140 mantras in a special Mantra Library view and sort them by day of the week or deity using filter controls. A Mantra Playback System 

(MantraDetailViewController + MantraAudioManager) turns the mantra collection into an audio playlist. You can add play, pause, seek, and track navigation controls like those on a music player to navigate the playlist. This enables users to move on to the next mantra or go back to the previous mantra within the active playlist. Third, individual mantra repetition events are recorded by the Digital Jaap Counter as screen tap gestures. A light haptic pulse is produced on each individual repetition by a three-tier UIImpactFeedbackGenerator mechanism, and a heavy haptic pulse is only produced upon mala completion (when the repetition count reaches the user-configured target, defaulting to 108). With each repetition, a circular arc progress indicator gradually fills in. The analytics engine benefits from the local persistence and cloud synchronization of cumulative mala counts. 

**8.** The Verse-Context Conversational AI Interface is the sixth inventive subsystem. This subsystem, which is implemented as VerseContextChatViewController, takes as input the chapter and verse number of a verse that is currently visible in the reading view. It then preseeds an AI conversation session with the context of that verse, focusing the AI mentor's initial response on the verse's specific instruction. This generates a conversational context that is intent-aware and does not require the user to manually describe the verse. 

**9.** The Multi-Dimensional Spiritual Analytics Engine is the seventh inventive subsystem. Over a rolling 90-day period, this subsystem aggregates user activity from five data streams: daily Jaap mala counts, per-verse reading progress (timestamp-indexed), challenge completion events, mood history (level and timestamp), and mantra listening (mantra ID and timestamp). For every data stream, a visual dashboard (StatsViewController) displays time-series bar and line graphs over user-selectable 7-day, 30-day, and 90-day windows. Dual-layer persistence uses a retry-safe task scheduler to write asynchronously to cloud database tables after first writing to on-device UserDefaults.

### **Offline-First Hybrid Persistence Architecture** 

**1.** The "write local first" principle governs all data writes in GitaConnect. The primary data store, UserDefaults, is accessible without a network connection. SupabaseManager encompasses all related cloud database operations.RetryTask() is an async/await closure that executes the cloud write and initiates automatic retries in the event of a network outage.. This guarantees: (i) full app functionality even when there is no internet connection; (ii) no data loss during temporary network failures; and (iii) eventual consistency as all local events converge to the cloud when connectivity is restored. 

**2.** LoginViewController manages user authentication through a cloud authentication service using a password and email address. The UUID of the authenticated user serves as the primary foreign key for each cloud database table. After you log in for the first time, StatsStore collects remote data records (like reading progress, mood history, and stats) and adds them to the stores on your device. ## **ABSTRACT** 

GitaConnect is an integrated iOS mobile application system for Bhagavad Gita-based spiritual guidance, practice, and analytics, comprising seven subsystems: (1) an AI Spiritual Mentor Module integrating the Sarvam-30B Indian large language model with a proprietary multi-rule prompt engineering framework enforcing language lock across 11 Indian regional languages, anti-hallucination safeguards verified against a device-resident 700-verse datastore, and regex-based verse-reference extraction and validation; (2) a Mood-Based Verse Recommendation Engine implementing a three-step emotional assessment with a real-time animated SwiftUI mood visualiser mapping pleasantness intensity to Bhagavad Gita verses via a structured JSON mapping table, with daily cloud-synchronised persistence; (3) a Spiritual Feed Module delivering a vertically scrollable reel-based stream of developer-curated Bhagavad Gita video content, with per-reel like, share, and learnings modal affordances via an “i” button; (4) a Gamified Spiritual Challenges Engine presenting daily, weekly, and milestonebased practice challenges with real-time progress computation, streak tracking, a cumulative Spiritual Level score, and an achievement badge gallery; (5) a Mantra Library, Playback System, and Digital Jaap Counter comprising a curated collection of 140 developer-provided mantras displayed on the Home Screen through a day-of-week deity-mapping mechanism (Monday → Shiva, Tuesday → Hanuman, etc.), a Mantra Library view filterable by deity or day, a music-player-style MantraAudioManager supporting play, pause, seek, and next/ previous playlist navigation across all 140 mantras, and a Digital Jaap Counter with three-tier variable haptic biofeedback providing light per-repetition haptic feedback and heavy haptic feedback exclusively at mala completion, with a circular arc progress indicator; (6) a VerseContext Conversational AI Interface pre-seeding AI sessions with the chapter and verse identifiers of the verse currently under study; and (7) a Multi-Dimensional Spiritual Analytics Engine aggregating mood trends, mantra listening, Jaap counts, reading progress, and challenge completions over a 90-day rolling window with visual dashboard rendering. The system has an offline-first hybrid persistence architecture that uses asynchronous retry-safe cloud database synchronisation and on-device UserDefaults as its main storage.
